# Combat, Milestone 3: Effects, the Rifle and the Sound of a Shot Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A shot is seen, lit and heard. Tracers fly. The muzzle flashes and lights the trench, brass spins out, and impacts throw dust coloured by what they hit. A rifle is drawn in the world's light. Shots, cracks and impacts have synthesised sound. And a visitor can climb onto the firing step and fire over the parapet.

**Architecture:**
- **Climbing.** The controller climbs firing-step timber that is walked into from the step (milestone 2's review: the bench was practically out of reach).
- **Events.** The session gathers what each tick's combat did — shots, the front's shots, impacts, cracks — until the frame drains them. Nothing is lost when a frame runs two ticks or none.
- **Sim side** (deterministic, `src/combat`):
  - the rifle's voxel model and the viewmodel's pose, which give the muzzle and the ejection port;
  - the particle pool, stepped per tick, which emits flash, brass, dust and sparks.
- **Render side:**
  - **effects:** a raster pass after sharpening draws tracers, flash stars and lit particles, depth-tested against the G-buffer;
  - **the flash's light:** a transient light added in `shade`, outside the denoiser;
  - **the rifle:** traced and shaded in its own pass after `shade`.
- **Sound.** `src/audio` turns drained events into synthesised WebAudio voices.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, WGSL, WebGPU (compute and one raster pipeline), WebAudio, Vitest, Playwright (Chrome).

## Global Constraints

- **Architecture rules** (tests/architecture.test.ts):
  - `core`, `world`, `physics`, `sim` and `combat` import nothing from `render`, `ui`, `app` or the new `audio`;
  - they use no DOM, WebGPU or WebAudio globals;
  - they use only the exactly rounded `Math` functions (`sqrt`, `min`, `max`, `floor`, `abs`, `PI`); anything else comes from `core/dmath`.
- **Every magnitude comes from the tunables** (data/tunables.toml). The spec's numbers are:
  - tracers up to 7 m;
  - a shot's light for 2 frames, about 0.5 m across, falling over 6 m;
  - a particle pool of 4,096;
  - the rifle at 1.5 cm voxels;
  - sound delayed at 343 m/s.
- **Determinism.** Particles are simulated in the session's tick and draw from the named stream `effects.particles`. The same seed gives the same brass.
- **The approved look holds.**
  - With nothing fired, no particles, effects, transient lights or rifle change a standard view.
  - The views and the check run are shown in fly mode, where the rifle is not drawn.
  - The GPU frame stays ≤ 14 ms at 1.5 MP in Chrome for every standard view at dusk and night.
- **Rendering conventions:**
  - G-buffer depth is distance along the unit ray, in voxels;
  - motion is the previous frame's pixel less this one's (`project`, unjittered);
  - effects are drawn after TAA, so unjittered, into `sharp` at `res`.
- **Commits** end with `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`. Work stays on branch `web/world`; nothing is pushed.

## Rulings made in planning

1. **The rifle is shaded in its own pass, not drawn into the G-buffer.** This reverses the spec's ruling in §4.
   - **Why:**
     - The G-buffer's hit word encodes world-axis faces and world cells.
     - The lighting, ReSTIR and denoising passes key their filters and history on world faces and planes, and reproject by world position. A rifle there would need its own normal encoding, plane identity and reprojection in each of them.
     - Even then it would be lit at the lighting grid's ½ or ¼ resolution, with a 32-frame history that a kicking close-up smears.
   - **Instead,** its pass (after `shade`, before TAA) traces the model per pixel and shades it from the world's own sources:
     - the key with `keyLit` (the key's height map, the trench's shadow per pixel);
     - the sky's irradiance, with a visibility traced through the world each frame from points along the rifle;
     - the point lights of the eye's light cell, each with a visibility traced from the rifle;
     - its own flash.
   - It writes colour, depth and motion, so TAA, the effects and bloom treat it as the world.
   - **Cost if wrong:** the sky's and the lanterns' shadows across the rifle are only as fine as those few probe points.
2. **A shot's light is a transient light added in `shade` and in the rifle's pass.** It is unshadowed, and stays out of the light buffer and the denoiser. This reverses §4's "through the light buffer".
   - **Why:** the denoiser averages up to 32 frames. A 2-frame light through ReSTIR would show at about 1/32 of its strength and linger for half a second. The flash sits about 1.2 m in front of the eye, so most of what the eye sees, it lights.
   - **Cost if wrong:** a flash can light a surface the muzzle cannot see. Milestone 4's blast light, 20–50 m off, needs a shadow, which its plan decides.
3. **Climbing onto the firing step.**
   - A player standing on firing-step timber who walks into a timber ledge climbs it, at `climb_speed`. The ledge must be more than a step-up and at most `climb_m` over their feet, with room to stand on it. A pinned, crouched or prone player does not climb.
   - The reference's controller has no climb, and its parity fixtures never stand on timber facing a timber ledge.
   - **Cost if wrong:** a timber ledge somewhere other than a bench can be climbed from timber.
4. **The flash and the brass leave from the drawn rifle's muzzle and ejection port.** These are the model's points under the viewmodel's pose. `ViewKick.muzzle()` and its `muzzle_*` keys are removed.
   - The reference's point (0.55 m out) lies on the same line of sight as its mesh's muzzle, at half the distance. The light, the flash and the tracer's start must agree with the drawn rifle.
   - **Cost if wrong:** none visible.
5. **The particle pool** is 4,096 (the spec), not the reference's 1,024. Dust and sparks are new kinds: `[particles.dust]` and `[particles.spark]`.

## Review Focus

- **A frame that runs no tick, or several.** Each shot gets one flash, one light, one brass and one report, and each impact one puff and one sound: none lost, none doubled. Pinned in Task 2, and in Task 5 through the effects frame.
- **A wall in the way.** The rifle pushed into a wall is cut by it (the world's depth wins). Tracers and particles behind a wall are hidden. Pinned in Tasks 5 and 7.
- **Fly mode, the periscope and the standard views.** The rifle is not drawn, and with nothing fired the views are unchanged. Pinned in Task 7.
- **A full pool.** Emission beyond 4,096 particles is refused and counted, without error. Pinned in Task 4.
- **Dynamic resolution and the low preset.** The effects' viewport and the rifle's screen box follow `res`, and the transient light works at light scale 4. Pinned in Tasks 5–7.
