# Light, Milestone 6: Proof Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Prove sub-project 2's done table in both browsers across the day. On the way, finish two things milestone 5 left: the flares' shafts (row 5's second half) and the low preset (§6).

**Architecture:**
- **Flare shafts.**
  - Each burning flare gets a shadow map of the hemisphere below it: 256² texels on a paraboloid, each texel the distance to the first voxel in that direction, out to the flare's radius.
  - The maps are traced a sixteenth a frame while the flare moves, and outright when it jumps (re-lit where it began).
  - The rays ride in the primary pass's `keyed` dispatch beside the key's, through one call site.
  - The froxels' point-light draw reads a flare's map, so its glow in the haze is cut by what stands between.
- **The low preset.**
  - The froxel grid is 80 × 45 (its columns read from the textures' size).
  - ReSTIR's spatial pass copies each pixel's own reservoir.
  - The sky-view LUT is 96 × 54.
- **The proof.**
  - The check (check.ts) measures every standard view at the four keys, and the overview through the day, in Chrome and in Safari.
  - The perf spec gates the day in Chrome.
  - The screenshot sets and the load close it.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, WGSL compute, Vitest, Playwright (Chrome), Safari through `npm run safari`.

## Rulings made in planning

- **A flare's map is the hemisphere below it, on a paraboloid.**
  - Flares hang over no man's land. Everything their light reaches that a voxel could block lies below them, and above them is open sky.
  - One 256² paraboloid texel spans about 0.9° straight below the flare and 0.45° toward its horizon: at 30 m, half a metre to a quarter.
  - Cost if wrong: a flare low enough to light a trench wall from below its own height gets no shadow above its horizon. That light then glows unshadowed, as it does now.
- **The flares' rays ride in the primary pass's `keyed` dispatch.**
  - They share the key's call site, so the shader keeps two traversals, not three.
  - The pass runs keyed on any frame either needs. Flares move always, so that is every frame at night.
  - Cost if wrong: the keyed pass's ~0.1 ms of code cost every night frame.
- **The low preset drops spatial reuse with the existing flag** (FLAG_SPATIAL_OFF: the spatial pass copies each pixel's own reservoir), not by skipping the pass.
  - The lighting pass reads one buffer either way.
  - Cost if wrong: about 0.05 ms of copying on low.

## Review Focus

- **A flare re-lit where it began, or guttering out.** The map follows the jump (an outright rebuild), with no shaft left from where it was. A flare at intensity 0 (out, or by day) is traced for nothing. Pinned in Task 0.
- **More burning flares than maps.** The ninth and later glow unshadowed, with no wrong map read for them. Pinned in Task 0.
- **A preset switched while running.** The froxels and the sky-view LUT are reallocated, the histories start afresh, and the scene group follows the new LUT. Pinned in Task 1.
- **Safari.** Where Safari lacks timestamp queries, the check times by submitted work, an overestimate the report names. A Safari-only failure in a shader or format must land in the report as an error, never as a hang. Pinned in Task 3.
- **The hour round sunset.** The sun 0–2° up makes the lighting pass's key rays skim; the frame stays within 14 ms there. Pinned in Task 2.
