# Battle, Milestone 5: Water Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Water's surface reflects the voxel world as it is lit, the sky beyond, and the flares and fires as glints; rounds and shells falling in water ripple it and are heard splashing, and the wind roughens it.

**Architecture:**
- **Where water is met (`src/world/raycast.ts`, `src/combat/ballistics.ts`, `src/sim/session.ts`):** a round's walk notes where it first enters water; a shell landing on water is a blast that struck water. Both are the session's `splashes`.
- **Ripples (`src/battle/water.ts`, pure; the director keeps them):** the app feeds the director the session's splashes, as it feeds strikes; each starts a ring on the battle's clock that spreads at 1 m/s and fades over 2 s; the 32 newest are the frame's. The wind's speed sets the surface's roughness. At rest the water is still.
- **Render side:**
  - a new compute pass, `reflect` (`src/render/reflections.ts`, `shaders/reflect.wgsl`), over the water tops the G-buffer holds: the surface's normal (rings and roughness), a reflected ray traced through the world with the shared traversal to 160 m and shaded cheaply, else the sky; the flares' and fires' glints along it; into a reflection texture;
  - the shade pass weighs that against the water's own dark colour by Fresnel, as it does the sky now;
  - the low preset reflects the sky and glints only.
- **Sound:** a splash where a round or a shell meets water.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, WGSL, WebGPU, WebAudio, Vitest, Playwright (Chrome).

**Spec:** docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-trenchline-web-battle-design.md:

## Global Constraints

- **Architecture rules** hold for `src/battle/`: no imports from `render`, `ui`, `app` or `audio`, and only the exactly rounded `Math` functions.
- **The spec's values:**
  - A reflected ray is traced up to 160 m, then the sky beyond.
  - Its hit is shaded by its albedo, the key light through the key shadow map, the sky's ambient, the nearest few lights of its light cell unshadowed, and its emission.
  - Flares and fires add their glints: where the reflected ray passes close to such a light, its disc.
  - A Fresnel term weighs the reflection against the water's own dark colour.
  - A ring spreads at 1 m/s and fades over 2 s; the surface takes the 32 newest, and the wind's roughness on top.
  - The low preset reflects the sky and glints only; the high preset traces the world.
  - The reflection costs ≤ 1 ms at no man's land at night.
  - A splash is heard where a round or a shell meets water.
- **Every magnitude comes from the tunables,** in a new `[battle.water]` table, `[render]` keys and `[audio]` keys.
- **Determinism.** The same seed, inputs (splashes among them) and steps give the same rings. Seeking replays no splashes, which it never had.
- **The look.** Water reflecting the world, not the sky alone, is the point of this milestone, so the views with water change; they are captured before and after for the user. Away from water, the views held at rest draw as before.
- **Commits** end with `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`. Work stays on `web/world`, and nothing is pushed.

## Rulings made in planning

1. **Where water is met:**
   - A round: its walk (`raycast`) already passes through water voxels; it now notes the first water voxel it enters from outside water, and the ballistics report a splash there once per round (a round already under water at its segment's start splashes no more).
   - A shell: `land` already takes the column's top, water counting; a blast whose struck material is water is a splash at the water's top as well as a blast.
   - Debris: the particles are cosmetic and never meet the world (combat spec), so debris starts no rings; a shell landing in water stands for its debris there.
2. **Ripples are the director's,** as the spec's §1 has the app feed it the water met:
   - each splash starts a ring at the battle's clock, its strength by its energy (a round's ~1 kJ small, a shell's large: `strength = clamp(log10(energy_j) / ring_log_full, 0.05, 1)`);
   - a ring's radius is `ring_speed_mps × age` and its height `strength × ring_height_m × (1 − age/ring_fade_s)²` until `ring_fade_s`;
   - the 32 newest alive are the frame's; older ones are dropped.
3. **The ring's shape:** a wave packet about its radius, `h(d) = A · cos(2π(d − r)/λ) · exp(−((d − r)/w)²)` with `λ = ring_wavelength_m` and `w = ring_width_m + 0.1 m × age`; the normal is the height's analytic gradient. Rings apply within 0.5 m of their water's height.
4. **Roughness:** two octaves of travelling waves along the wind and one across it, their slope `roughness_per_mps × |wind|`; the wind is the director's at its moment. At rest (no battle), the water is still: a mirror.
5. **The reflection pass** (`reflect`, after the froxels, before shade):
   - a compute pass over the internal image, each thread returning at once unless its G-buffer pixel is a water voxel's top face;
   - the reflected ray leaves 1/16 voxel above the surface along `reflect(rd, n)`, its `y` kept ≥ 0.02 (a perturbed normal never sends it under the water), and is traced with `traceWorld` to `reflect_m` (160 m);
   - a hit is shaded per the spec, plus the analytic fog over the reflected path; a miss is the sky (`skyWithBodies`);
   - the output is the reflected radiance (rgba16float, the internal image's size);
   - the low preset skips the trace.
6. **Glints:**
   - the frame's flares and fires (each light of kind `'flare'` or `'fire'` the frame lights; the six at rest are flares) are its glints, at most 32, the brightest first;
   - along the reflected ray, a glint's disc of `glint_m` (flares) or `glint_fire_m` (fires) adds its light, `colour × intensity × glint_gain / (distance² + 1)`, with a soft edge over the disc's outer quarter;
   - the frame uniform takes them (position, disc radius, radiance).
7. **The water's own colour:** as now, `albedo × lit × 0.6 + fresnel × reflection`, with the reflection from the pass in place of the sky.
8. **The splash:** a round's is a short bandpassed "plip" (1.8 kHz, 60 ms) heard within `impact_within_m`; a shell's a lowpassed rush of noise over a second with a thump, heard at any distance late by it, as a blast is.

## Review Focus

- **A crater's water at night under a flare.** In no man's land at night, a flooded crater shows the flare's glint where the flare's mirror image lies, the lit lips of the crater reflected about it, and the sky beyond; and it costs ≤ 1 ms. Pinned in Tasks 4 and 7.
- **Rest still.** At rest the water has no rings and no roughness, and away from water the views draw as before. Pinned in Tasks 2, 4 and 7.
- **Rings.** A round into a crater's water starts a ring there that spreads at 1 m/s and is gone by 2 s; a shell's is larger; at most the 32 newest show. Pinned in Tasks 1, 2 and 5.
- **The low preset.** It reflects the sky and the glints, traces nothing, and costs next to nothing. Pinned in Task 4.
- **Grazing views.** Seen nearly edge-on, the reflection is the strongest (Fresnel) and the perturbed normal never sends a ray under the surface. Pinned in Tasks 4 and 5.
