# Milestone 4: the renderer — Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Ray-trace the generated 10 cm world in the browser at dusk and at night within 14 ms of GPU time at 1.5 MP on the M1 Pro. The frame covers primary visibility, quarter-count lighting, denoising, shading, the far field, TAA and present.

**Architecture:**
- **Compute-first WebGPU.** The world's brick table, sector mask, compressed brick heap, palette and a max-height pyramid live in storage buffers. One hierarchical DDA in WGSL serves every ray.
- **The frame:** primary → lighting (one representative per 2×2 block) → temporal accumulation → three à-trous passes → upsample and shade → TAA → CAS → present.
- **Testing:**
  - Vitest covers pure logic: layouts, camera, pyramid, uploads, keyframes, lights and dynamic resolution.
  - Shaders run in headless Chrome through a Playwright harness and are checked against CPU references.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript 7 (strict), WebGPU and WGSL, Vite 8 (with a WGSL include plugin), Vitest 5, and Playwright 1.64 driving the installed Chrome.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-trenchline-web-world-design.md`. This plan implements:
- §3 Rendering;
- §2 Storage (Edits) and §2 Far field;
- from §4 App, the debug overlay and capture hooks;
- from Testing, the Playwright smoke and perf runs.

## Global Constraints

- **Compute first.** Every pass is a compute shader except the final present (spec §3).
- **No runtime dependencies** (spec §1).
- **Imports.** `src/render`, `src/ui` and `src/app` may import `core` and `world`. Nothing in `core`, `world`, `physics` or `sim` imports them; `tests/architecture.test.ts` enforces this.
- **GPU budget.** The GPU frame is ≤ 14 ms on the M1 Pro at the "high" preset's internal resolution (1.5 MP), with dynamic resolution off. This holds for each standard view:
  - front-line bay along the trench;
  - firing step looking out;
  - no man's land at night;
  - the elevated overview;
  - inside a dugout;
  - the hamlet.
- **Load budget.** From navigation to ready is ≤ 4 s in Chrome (criterion 1). This milestone must not push the measured page past it.
- **Camera conventions:**
  - yaw 0 looks down +z;
  - right is (cos ψ, 0, −sin ψ);
  - angles are in degrees;
  - the horizontal field of view is 90°.
- **Lights.** At most 64 point lights.
- **Fixed techniques** (spec §3):
  - history length capped at 32;
  - three à-trous passes;
  - Halton (2,3) jitter;
  - YCoCg neighbourhood clamp;
  - ACES fit;
  - blue-noise dither.
- **Resolution.** Dynamic resolution runs between 50% and 100% per axis. The "high" preset is the default; "low" adds quarter-resolution lighting.
- **Shader build and timing.** WGSL modules are composed from shared files by a build-time include step. Each pass reports its GPU time to the F3 overlay.
- **The approved look** (the probe the user signed off) is binding at the dusk and night keys, including its gamma-2.2 output encode:

  | Key | Light | Sky zenith | Sky horizon | Ambient | Fog (/m) | Exposure |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Dusk | sun at azimuth 18°, elevation 4.5°, colour (3.4, 1.45, 0.6) | (0.1, 0.13, 0.27) | (0.95, 0.46, 0.24) | 0.55 | 0.012 | 1.0 |
  | Night | moon at azimuth 215°, elevation 32°, colour (0.05, 0.07, 0.12) | (0.004, 0.006, 0.016) | (0.03, 0.025, 0.04) | 1.0 | 0.02 | 3.2 |

- **Texture formats.** Storage textures use only core formats: r32float, r32uint, rg32float, rg32uint, rgba16float, rgba32float, rgba8unorm. A pass reads earlier outputs as sampled textures, never as read-write storage textures, which both Chrome and Safari 26 accept.
- **Commits** end with `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`.

## Review Focus

- **Window resize and devicePixelRatio changes mid-run.** Textures reallocate and histories reset, with no GPU validation error and no garbage frame. Pinned in Task 10.
- **Cameras outside the volume or on its boundary planes, and axis-parallel rays.**
  - The overview starts above the volume.
  - Rays with zero direction components, and origins exactly on brick or sector planes, must neither loop nor miss.
  - Pinned in Task 3.
- **No WebGPU, a lost device, or adapter limits below what the world needs (Safari).** The page shows a clear message, never a hang or a blank page. Pinned in Task 10.
- **Long frames and hidden tabs.** A paused requestAnimationFrame must not wreck dynamic resolution or leave TAA blending a stale history. Pinned in Task 10.
- **Edits arriving faster than the upload budget.**
  - No write is lost or reordered.
  - The GPU table never points at a record not yet written.
  - The sector mask and pyramid stay conservative, and become exact once the queue drains.
  - Pinned in Task 2.

**Units.**
- Inside WGSL every position and distance is in voxels. `Frame.voxel` (0.1) converts to metres where the formulas below are in metres.
- TypeScript poses are in metres and degrees.

**Bind groups.** Every compute pass uses one explicit layout for group 0, the scene. It is declared once in `scene.wgsl`:

| Binding | Resource |
|---|---|
| 0 | `frame` uniform |
| 1 | `table` |
| 2 | `sectors` |
| 3 | `heap` |
| 4 | `palette` (array<vec4f>: albedo + roughness, then emission + kind) |
| 5 | `pyramid` |
| 6 | `lights` |
| 7 | far-field heights (texture_2d<f32>) |
| 8 | far-field max mips (texture_2d<f32>, all levels) |
| 9 | far-field materials (texture_2d<u32>) |
| 10 | blue noise (texture_2d<u32>) |

Group 1 is per pass. It holds sampled inputs and write-only storage outputs, with explicit layouts.

**Frame flags** (`Frame.flags` bits): `JITTER_OFF` 1, `PYRAMID_OFF` 2, `HISTORY_RESET` 4, `DEBUG_SHADE` 8.

  - `tests/render/pyramid.test.ts`, `tests/render/uploads.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/world.spec.ts`

**Why.** Encoding the full world on the main thread takes ~1 s (measured: 342,976 dense bricks → 60.5 MB, 176 B average, formats [13.8k, 220k, 70k, 39k, 0]). The spec has workers return encoded records, so each band encodes its own.

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: `BandResult`, `HeapMirror`, `encodeBrick`, `tableEntry`, `BrickMap.sectorMask()`, `paletteData()`.
- Produces:
  - **`BandResult`** gains:
    - `records: Uint32Array` — the `encodeBrick` records of `denseBricks`, concatenated;
    - `formats: Uint8Array` — one record format per dense brick.
  - **`HeapMirror.fromBands(map, bands)`** takes the bands sorted by band, on a map assembled from them and not yet edited.
    - It copies each band's records with one `set`.
    - Uniform and empty entries come from `map.table`.
  - **Edits.** `HeapMirror.update(bricks)` now records `BrickEdit { brick: number; entry: number; unit: number; words: Uint32Array | null; retire: number }`, read back with `takeEdits(): BrickEdit[]`.
    - `takeEdits` replaces `takeWrites` and `takeTableWrites`.
    - `retire` is the replaced dense entry, or 0.
    - A retired record's units are reused only after `release(entry: number)`.
  - **`GeneratedWorld`** gains `mirror: HeapMirror`.
    - The mirror is built from the bands, then updated with the bricks the events dirtied.
    - Its pending edits are cleared.
    - Timings gain `encode`, the main-thread part.
  - **`interface Pyramid { levels: { w: number; h: number; offset: number }[]; data: Uint32Array }`**
    - Level 0 holds one value per brick column, x fastest: 1 + the highest non-air voxel's y in that column, or 0.
    - Each level above is the max over 2×2 cells (sizes `ceil(w/2)`), down to 1×1.
  - `buildPyramid(map): Pyramid`; `updatePyramid(p, map, bricks): number[]` returns the `data` indices that changed.
  - **`interface GpuSink { table(i: number, entry: number): void; heap(unit: number, words: Uint32Array): void; sector(i: number, v: number): void; pyramid(i: number, v: number): void }`**
  - **`class UploadQueue`** —
    - `constructor(map, mirror, pyramid, gpuState: { sectors: Uint32Array; pyramid: Uint32Array })`
    - `push(edits: readonly BrickEdit[]): void`
    - `flush(budgetBytes: number, eye: [number, number, number], sink: GpuSink): number`
    - `get pending(): number`
    - Rules:
      - **Coalescing.** Edits coalesce per brick. A superseded edit's record never reached the GPU, so it is released at once, and the surviving edit keeps the oldest `retire`.
      - **Order.** `flush` uploads whole edits nearest-first, by brick centre to `eye` in voxels, until the budget is spent. It always uploads at least one.
      - **Per edit:** record words first, then the table entry, then `mirror.release(retire)`.
      - **Sector bits** are set as soon as an edit makes a brick non-empty. They are never cleared during play.
      - **Pyramid values** rise immediately. A column's values fall only once that column has no pending edits.
  - **`class GpuWorld`:**
    - `static create(device, world: { map: BrickMap; mirror: HeapMirror; pyramid: Pyramid }, opts?: { headroom?: number }): GpuWorld`
    - Buffers: `table`, `sectors`, `heap`, `palette`, `pyramid`.
    - `queue: UploadQueue`; `flush(budgetBytes, eye)`.
    - `generation: number` bumps when the heap is reallocated, so bind groups are rebuilt.
    - The heap is allocated at used × (1 + headroom), with headroom 0.25 by default. It grows ×1.5 by reallocate-and-copy when an edit's unit lies beyond it.
  - **`syntheticWorld(seed): BrickMap`** is 256 × 128 × 256 voxels. It contains:
    - uniform ground below y 40;
    - a noisy dense surface between 40 and 48;
    - walls with openings;
    - a roof slab;
    - single-voxel wires;
    - a water pool;
    - one brick of 20 materials (format 4);
    - empty sectors overhead.
  - **Tunables:** `[render] upload_budget_kib = 1024`.

### Task 3: One traversal for every ray

**Files:**
- Create: `src/render/shaders/scene.wgsl`, `common.wgsl`, `palette.wgsl`, `traverse.wgsl`, `tests/support/trace-ref.ts`, `tests/e2e/shaders/trace-test.wgsl`, `tests/e2e/cases/traverse.ts`
- Test: `tests/render/trace-ref.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/traverse.spec.ts`

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: the `GpuWorld` buffers; the `Pyramid` levels (passed in `Frame.pyramid`, an array of 12 `vec4u` holding w, h, offset, 0); `Frame.world` (dims in voxels); the Task 1 layout.
- Produces:
  - **WGSL `struct Hit { hit: bool, t: f32, cell: vec3i, face: u32, mat: u32, steps: u32 }`**
    - `t` is in voxels along a unit direction.
    - `face` is 0–5 for −x, +x, −y, +y, −z, +z: the face of `cell` the ray came in through.
  - **`fn traceWorld(ro: vec3f, rd: vec3f, tMax: f32, wantMaterial: bool) -> Hit`**
    - It clips to [0, dims).
    - Hierarchical DDA: an empty sector skips 32 voxels; an empty brick skips 8; a uniform brick is a hit; a dense brick steps voxels through the occupancy mask.
    - **Pyramid skip.** Before skipping an empty sector or brick, or entering a dense brick, it tries the pyramid. It starts at level `min(levels − 1, 6)` and goes down. A pyramid cell is skipped when the ray's y, both where it enters and where it leaves that cell in xz, is ≥ the cell's value.
    - `wantMaterial = false` reads only table, sector, mask and pyramid words.
    - It gives up after `MAX_STEPS = 2048`, reporting a miss with `steps = 2048`.
    - `PYRAMID_OFF` disables the skip.
  - **`fn decodeMaterial(entry: u32, li: u32) -> u32`** follows `encode.ts`:
    - format = (entry >> 28) & 7; unit = entry & 0x0fffffff;
    - mask word = heap[unit·4 + 4 + (li >> 5)];
    - data starts at word 20;
    - bits per voxel are [0, 1, 2, 4, 8] by format;
    - local palette ids sit in words 0–3.
  - **`fn faceNormal(face: u32) -> vec3f`.**
  - **`traceRef(map, ro, rd, tMax): { hit: boolean; t: number; cell: [number, number, number]; face: number; mat: number }`** — an f64 Amanatides–Woo walk over every voxel with `map.get`, using the same clip and face conventions.

**Interfaces:**
- Consumes: `FarField` (`size` 2048, `cell` 20 voxels, `originX`/`originZ`, `height` in voxels, with −Infinity inside the map, and `material`).
- Produces:
  - **`farFieldMips(ff): Float32Array[]`**
    - Level 0 cell (i, j) holds the max of the vertex heights (i..i+1, j..j+1). The last row and column clamp.
    - Each level up is the max over 2×2 cells. A 2048 field gives 12 levels, down to 1×1.
    - −Infinity survives.
  - **`FarFieldGpu.create(device, ff)`:**
    - heights: r32float 2048², the vertices;
    - mips: an r32float texture with 12 mip levels;
    - materials: r8uint, sampled as `texture_2d<u32>`.
  - **`fn marchFar(ro: vec3f, rd: vec3f, tMin: f32, tMax: f32) -> FarHit { hit: bool, t: f32, n: vec3f, mat: u32 }`** — a quadtree max-mip march:
    - At a level-k cell, if the ray's lowest y across its crossing of that cell is ≥ the cell's max, it advances to the cell's exit and climbs a level when aligned. Otherwise it descends.
    - At level 0, where the crossing can be below the max, the segment is tested against the bilinear patch of the four vertices. When the endpoints straddle the patch, 6 bisection steps refine the hit.
    - The normal comes from the patch's gradient.
  - **In primary:**
    - A ray that never enters the volume, or leaves it without a hit, marches the far field from where it left (or from 0) to 2.2 km.
    - A far hit writes face 6, its t, and a palette id: FIELD and NONE → SOD, PLOUGHED → MUD_TURNED, WOOD → BRANCH, ROOF → BRICK_DARK, WALL → BRICK, SPIRE → CONCRETE.
    - word0 holds the far cell's i | j << 12, and word1's y is 0.
  - **`marchRef(ff, ro, rd, tMax)`** steps 0.05 cell at a time over the bilinear surface, in f64, and bisects the hit.

### Task 7: Denoise

**Files:**
- Create: `src/render/shaders/temporal.wgsl`, `src/render/shaders/atrous.wgsl`, `tests/e2e/cases/denoise.ts`

**Interfaces and the algorithm** (pinned):
- **History**, at the lighting resolution and ping-ponged:
  - `hColor` rgba16float: rgb, with a = key visibility;
  - `hMoments` rgba16float: m1, m2, length;
  - the previous frame's `hDepth` and `hHit`.
- **Temporal.**
  - **Reprojection.** The representative's world point is projected with `Frame.prev` (unjittered), divided by L, and offset by −0.5. That gives four bilinear taps into the previous grid.
  - **Tap validity.** A tap is valid when its face equals the current face and |n·(P_tap − P)| < 0.5 voxel. Each P is rebuilt from its own depth and its own frame's representative ray.
  - **Blending.**
    - Weights are bilinear × valid, renormalised. With no valid tap, the length is 0.
    - length′ = min(length + 1, `history_max`), and α = 1/length′.
    - Colour and moments blend with α over l = lum(rgb) + a·lum(keyColour)·N·L.
  - **Variance.** max(m2 − m1², 0) when length′ ≥ 4. Otherwise it is the 3 × 3 spatial variance of l over valid neighbours (same face, and passing the plane test).
  - `HISTORY_RESET` makes every length 0.
- **À-trous.** Three passes with steps 1, 2 and 4, using a 5 × 5 B3 kernel.
  - **Weights.** A neighbour on a different face gets 0. Otherwise:
    - w_plane = exp(−|n·(P_q − P_p)| / `atrous_sigma_plane`);
    - w_lum = exp(−|l_p − l_q| / (`atrous_sigma_luma`·√(g3×3(var_p)) + 1e-4)).
  - **Outputs.** Colour = Σwc/Σw, and variance = Σw²·var/(Σw)².
  - **Feedback.** The first pass's output is written back into this frame's `hColor`. The third pass's output feeds shading.

  - History comes from the previous TAA output at (pixel + 0.5 + motion)/res, with 9-tap Catmull-Rom. Off-screen means there is no history.
  - History is clamped to the 3 × 3 YCoCg AABB of the current shaded colour.
  - The blend runs in Karis-weighted space (w = 1/(1 + luma)), with α = clamp(0.06 + 0.04·|motion| px, 0.06, 0.25).
  - α = 1 with no history or with `HISTORY_RESET`.
- **CAS** (compute, internal resolution), AMD FidelityFX CAS:
  - amp = √(saturate(min(mn, 1 − mx)/mx)), with mn and mx the min and max over the cross;
  - w = amp·(−1/lerp(8, 5, `cas_sharpness`));
  - out = (w·(N + S + E + W) + C)/(1 + 4w).
  - It works on c/(1 + max(c)) and inverts the result.
- **Present** (a render pass over a full-screen triangle):
  - Catmull-Rom sample of the CAS output at the canvas pixel, mapped to internal resolution.
  - × exposure.
  - ACES fit: x(2.51x + 0.03)/(x(2.43x + 0.59) + 0.14).
  - Gamma 2.2 encode.
  - Dither: + (blue/4096 − 0.5)/255.
  - The canvas is in the preferred format, alpha opaque.

**Pinned:**
- **Presets.**
  - high: internal width = round(√(1.5e6·aspect)) and height = round(width/aspect), with lighting scale 2.
  - low: the same internal target with lighting scale 4.
- **Dynamic resolution.**
  - **Range.** Axis scale s ∈ [0.5, 1], in steps of 1/32.
  - **Measurement.** An EMA (α 0.1) of the GPU frame ms. Without timestamps it uses frame intervals; intervals > 250 ms are ignored.
  - **Decision,** every 30 frames:
    - when ema > budget·1.05, s ← max(0.5, quantise(s·√(budget/ema)));
    - when ema < budget·0.8, s rises by one step.
  - Textures are allocated at s = 1, and passes use the scaled size. A scale change does not reset histories.
- **Frame gaps.** A gap > 500 ms between rendered frames sets `HISTORY_RESET` for one frame.
- **Resize.** The canvas is client size × min(devicePixelRatio, 2). Reallocation is debounced by 150 ms and resets histories.
- **Device failures:**
  - `checkLimits(limits, need): string[]` lists shortfalls; a non-empty list throws `GpuUnavailable('limits')`.
  - `device.lost` shows "The graphics device was lost (reason). Reload the page to continue." and stops the loop.
  - With no WebGPU, the page shows "TRENCHLINE needs WebGPU: use Chrome, Edge or Safari 26."
- **Overlay (F3):**
  - performance: per-pass GPU ms (EMA), total GPU ms, fps;
  - resolution: internal resolution and scale, and preset;
  - memory: dense bricks, heap MB (used and allocated), table MB, pending uploads;
  - camera: position (m), yaw and pitch, time of day;
  - location: the bay, from `bayIndex(plan).at(x, z)`, e.g. "front bay 12".
- **Keys** (until milestone 5):
  - click locks the pointer;
  - W A S D, Space and C fly;
  - Shift multiplies speed by 4;
  - 1–6 jump to the views;
  - T cycles time through 0 → 0.5 → 1;
  - [ and ] step time by ±0.05;
  - P toggles the preset; R toggles dynamic resolution;
  - F3 toggles the overlay.
- **Hooks** add `setQuality({ preset?, dynres?, scale? })`, `loseDevice()`, and `simulateGap(ms)`. `stats()` also reports `scale`, `preset` and `reset`.
