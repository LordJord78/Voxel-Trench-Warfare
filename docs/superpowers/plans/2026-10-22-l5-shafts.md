# Light, Milestone 5: Shafts Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:**
- Let the haze show the key light's shafts through gaps (row 5).
- Keep every standard view within the 14 ms budget (row 1) and the approved look.

**Architecture:**
- **Headroom first.** The key light's shadow ray costs the overview 1.2–2.0 ms a frame (measured: dusk 1.90, night 1.65, noon 1.17, dawn 2.01), and the overview stands at 13.7–14.0 ms. Each lighting pixel will trace the key or the sky by turns, as the sky ray already takes turns, and the denoiser will keep the key's visibility as the average of the samples it had.
- **A light-space height map** of the key light, 1,024² r16float.
  - Each texel holds how far along the light a ray from above the map runs before the voxel world blocks it.
  - The map lies in a plane square to the light and covers the map's own volume seen along the light, its texels 0.25 m or as much more as the low sun's longer reach needs.
  - It is rebuilt a sixteenth a frame, and outright when the key changes between sun and moon.
- **Froxels.** A 160 × 90 × 64 grid over the frustum, its slices exponential in depth from 0.5 m to 120 m.
  - Each froxel holds the haze's extinction there and the light it scatters toward the eye: the key light (shadowed by the height map), the sky's and the front's glow, and four point lights drawn as ReSTIR's candidates are.
  - It is jittered in depth each frame and blended with last frame's, reprojected.
- **Integration.** A front-to-back pass turns the froxels into in-scattering and transmittance at each slice. Shading reads them at the pixel's depth in place of the analytic fog nearer than 120 m; the analytic fog carries on beyond.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript, WGSL compute, 3D storage textures, Vitest, Playwright GPU cases.

**Spec:** docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-trenchline-web-light-design.md (§5 Shafts; done-table rows 1 and 5; row 6's load).

## Global Constraints

- **Height map:** 1,024 × 1,024, r16float; rebuilt over 16 frames, 64k rays a frame, interleaved; outright on a sun/moon switch.
- **Froxels:** 160 × 90 × 64; exponential in depth to 120 m; jittered in depth per frame and blended with the reprojected froxels from last frame; four point lights per froxel, chosen as ReSTIR's candidates are.
- **Integration:** front to back; shading reads it at each pixel's depth in place of the analytic fog nearer than 120 m; beyond, the analytic fog continues the same falloff.
- **Perf:** every standard view ≤ 14 ms at dusk, night, noon and dawn (1.5 MP, dynamic resolution off). The shafts all told ≤ 0.6 ms on the overview.
- **The approved look:** with nothing to cast a shaft, the froxel haze is the analytic haze (milestones 2 and 3's fit), so the approved views' exposures and region means hold.
- **Load:** ≤ 4 s (Chrome).
- Commits end with `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`.

## Rulings made in planning

- **The shafts' phase is the approved haze's.** That is `hazeKey + hazeForward · cos⁴`, fitted in milestones 2 and 3 to the approved frames, for the key light and for point lights alike. It replaces the spec's Henyey–Greenstein lobes (g 0.6 sun, 0.3 moon), so that the unshadowed haze stays exactly the approved haze. Cost if wrong: the shafts' brightness toward and away from the light differs from a physical lobe.
- **The height map is anchored to the map in light space, not to world x and z.** A world-aligned grid cannot hold a low sun's shadows: at 6° a point 16 m up shades ground 150 m away, beyond a 256 m grid. Its texels are 0.25 m where the map's light-space extent allows, larger at a low sun (a cap of 2 m). Cost if wrong: blurrier shafts at dawn and dusk.
- **Point lights scatter unshadowed.** The spec gives froxels no point-light visibility, and a shadow ray per froxel would cost some 3 ms. So a flare's glow in the haze is not cut by a parapet: under a flare, row 5's shafts are the key's, the moon's at night. Cost if wrong: a faint glow in a trench's haze from a flare its walls hide, and no flare shafts; the final report says so.

## Review Focus

- **A low or vanishing key.** Near the horizon the map's light-space extent runs away, and in twilight the key hands between sun and moon. Expect no NaN, a texel cap rather than a blow-up, and shafts that fade with the key. Pinned in Task 1 (the map at 1° elevation, and the switch).
- **The camera moving or cutting.** Last frame's froxels are reprojected only where they were in the frustum; a cut starts them afresh. Expect no ghost shafts trailing a turn. Pinned in Task 2 (a turned camera's froxels match a fresh camera's).
- **Points outside the height map.** The map covers the map's volume; anything beyond it, such as the far field or high sky, counts as lit, never shadowed by a clamped texel. Pinned in Task 1.
- **The 120 m boundary and the sky.** Haze from the froxels and from the analytic fog meet without a seam, and sky pixels take the volume's far slice. Pinned in Task 3 (a ramp of depths across 120 m is continuous).
- **The budget.** The overview had 0.3 ms or less in hand. Task 0 must free at least 0.6 ms, measured by A/B; every task runs perf.
